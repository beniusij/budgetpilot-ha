# Changelog

## [0.3.63] — 2026-09-17

### Added

- **The find shortcut now works inside dialogs that have their own filter.** Pressing ⌘F while the "Manual link" dialog is open jumps straight to its filter box and selects what is there, so you can type over it. It previously did nothing in the desktop app, which has no browser find bar to fall back on.

- **Taupa now runs as a native macOS app**, opening in its own window rather than a browser tab. It reads the same household data as before, and everything still runs on your own machine — the app starts and stops its own background service — including when the app closes unexpectedly, so nothing is left running behind your back — and that service is reachable only from the machine it is running on. The window remembers its size and position between launches, links to other websites open in your normal browser rather than taking over the app, and opening Taupa again while it is already running brings the existing window forward instead of starting a second copy.
- **A Resources section in the sidebar**, holding plain-language guides to what Taupa's figures actually mean — starting with the one the dashboard leads on. *What "free to spend" means* explains the figure as the subtraction it is, with a diagram: the cash in your current accounts, less what your pay-in-full cards already owe, less what your budgets are still holding back for the rest of the month. It also covers the two things people most often expect it to do and it does not — subtract your savings goals, and match your bank before you have set a start month.
- **Four articles on budgeting itself**, alongside the guides to Taupa's own figures. *Splitting the bills when you earn different amounts* compares an even split against one weighted by income, and shows what each costs the lower earner as a share of their pay. *Saving for the bills that don't come every month* covers sinking funds — how to size one, and why Taupa expects you to move real money into a real savings account rather than holding back a number that can drift. *The 50/30/20 rule, and where it goes wrong* explains the shape and the labelling mistake that quietly breaks it. *Which debt to clear first* sets out the highest-rate and smallest-balance orders, with the arithmetic that decides between them. Each one is illustrated, and each says plainly what Taupa does and does not do with the subject.
- **A guide to where your bank data goes**, for anyone hesitating before handing a budgeting app a statement. It sets out what actually happens to the file — read on your own machine, never uploaded, never stored, with only the rows you confirm kept — and names the one thing that does leave: a ticker or fund identifier sent to a market-data service, and only if you use investment tracking. It also says plainly what would change that, and that neither household sync nor automatic bank import exists yet. A closing section covers the part Taupa cannot do for you: the statement sitting in your Downloads folder.
