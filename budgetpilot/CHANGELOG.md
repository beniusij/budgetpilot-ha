# Changelog

## [0.3.56] — 2026-09-01

### Added

- **If you share bills but keep your money separate, the app now keeps a running tally of what one of you owes the other — and settles it in a couple of clicks.** In that arrangement one person inevitably pays for something you both used out of their own account: a shop, a repair, a weekend away. Until now there was nowhere to put that, so the debt lived in your heads or in a message thread. The person who paid can now **claim** the other's share straight from the payment on the Transactions page — say how much of it was theirs, or tap **50%** and the rest of the common splits to fill it in — and it joins a balance on a new **Settlement** card on the dashboard, which tells you who owes whom, how much, or simply that you are square.
- **What the other person is shown is deliberately narrow.** They see the claim itself — what it was for, the month it covers, and the full amount it was a share of, so they can check that the £28 they are being asked for really is a third of £84 — and nothing else. The payment stays on your own account, private, exactly as it was before. The claim keeps its own copy of that detail, so editing or deleting the original payment afterwards leaves it untouched.
- **Settling up then looks for the transfer in your own ledger.** After you record a settlement the app looks for a payment of the same amount within a week of the date you gave. If exactly one matches, it is linked for you; if two or three look alike, it asks which, closest date first; if nothing has arrived yet the settlement waits, marked "not yet seen in your ledger", and you can ask it to look again once your statement has imported. A settlement can also be unlinked from the wrong payment, or removed altogether if you recorded it by mistake. There is a **settled in cash** option for a settle-up that will never show in a bank statement. Matching only labels which payment cleared a debt — it never moves a figure. At month close you can also tick **Mark as settled when closing** to clear the whole outstanding balance in one go.
- **Nothing here starts on its own.** The balance is zero until somebody deliberately raises a claim, and a household that never raises one is never asked to settle anything. Paying more than you owed simply turns the balance around, and a claim made after you have settled re-opens it — there is no separate "settled up to" date to go stale.
- **A shared bill can also record what each of you owes on it**, stated in pounds rather than as a percentage: this arrangement divides nothing by a ratio, so the figures are ones you state, and anything left over stays unassigned rather than being split for you. Those figures are a record only — they change no budget, no spending total and no free-to-spend figure. You can claim the other person's share of a bill from the bill itself, and it is recorded at what the bill cost in the month you are looking at — so stepping back to July claims July's figure even if the price has risen since, and a month the bill was paused offers nothing to claim.
- All of the above appears only for households that share bills while keeping their money separate. Nothing changes for a household that pools its income or splits costs proportionally.
- On a bill, the per-member **Split between** fields now start folded away behind a toggle — most bills are paid whole by one person and never need them. If a share is waiting to be claimed, the folded heading says so, so nothing actionable is hidden without a word.
